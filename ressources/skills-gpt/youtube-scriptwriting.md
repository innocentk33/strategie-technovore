# YouTube Script Writing

Transform disorganized ideas into retention-optimized scripts through a checkpoint-based workflow.

## Core Psychology

All great scripts operate on one principle: **Reality must beat expectations.**

- Viewer expectations = what they think will happen based on title/thumbnail
- Reality = what you actually deliver in the video
- When reality > expectations → satisfaction, engagement, shares
- When reality < expectations → disappointment, drop-off, bounce

Every decision in scriptwriting serves this gap. You cannot beat expectations if you don't know what they are — which is why packaging comes before writing.

## Writing Style: Avoid AI-isms

Scripts must sound like a human creator talking, not like generated text. **Never use these formulations** (in English or French):

- Negation-contrast constructions: "It's not because A, but because B" / "Pas parce que A, mais parce que B"
- "It's not just X, it's Y" / "C'est pas juste Y, c'est X"
- Absolution openers: "It's not your fault, it's..." / "C'est pas de ta faute, c'est..."
- Repeated, heavy-handed hammering of "pain points" — agitating the viewer's problem once is a hook; doing it every paragraph is a tell

Also avoid: rule-of-three lists everywhere, "Here's the thing:", "Let that sink in", em-dash-heavy dramatic pauses, and symmetrical parallel sentences stacked back to back. Prefer plain, direct statements, varied sentence length, and concrete examples over rhetorical scaffolding.

Run a final pass on every script specifically hunting for these patterns and rewrite them as direct statements.

## The Workflow

Work through these checkpoints in order. Each builds on the previous. **Steps 1–3 (packaging, research, outline) happen before writing a single line of script.** Measure twice, cut once: if the idea or outline isn't strong, don't write the script — go back and research more.

### Checkpoint 1: Packaging (Idea, Title, Thumbnail)

The packaging sets the viewer's expectations, and the script's job is to confirm and then beat them.

1. **Idea**: One sentence describing the video. It should either solve a real pain point for the ideal viewer or open a rabbit hole they want to go down.
2. **Title**: Must be locked BEFORE writing the intro. The title triggers the click-curiosity loop and defines the incoming expectation. Slight wording tweaks change the expectation completely — you can't write click confirmation without knowing the exact title.
3. **Thumbnail**: A loose concept is enough at this stage. Don't let thumbnail iteration block scriptwriting; it gets dialed in later.
4. **Target audience & desired emotion**: Who specifically is watching, what do they already know, and which ONE primary emotion is the video transferring (awe, amusement, excitement, outrage, surprise/curiosity, empathy)?

### Checkpoint 2: Research & Mine for Shock

Find the facts that will beat expectations.

**Shock Score Method**: Rate each fact 1-100 based on "How many viewers would NOT know this?"
- 80+ = Gold (most viewers surprised)
- 50-79 = Good supporting points
- Below 50 = Skip unless essential context

Collect 5-10 high-shock facts before proceeding.

### Checkpoint 3: Outline First — the Uniqueness Gut-Check

Before any prose, write a quick-and-dirty bulleted outline of the body points. The intro is formulaic and can always be solved later; the real moat of a video is whether the body says something genuinely unique.

For each bullet, layer on:
- **What**: what the point is
- **Why**: why it matters
- **How**: how it fits into the overall story

Then zoom out and gut-check: **is this actually unique, or am I summarizing what everyone already says?** Uniqueness can come from the tip itself, the way it's distilled, or the example used to explain it. Viewers' expectations are built from existing common knowledge — repeating common knowledge can never beat them.

If the points aren't unique: do NOT proceed to writing. Research more source material and come back. Also decide the ordering here (see Checkpoint 5) and mark which point naturally suits a native CTA embed (see Checkpoint 7).

### Checkpoint 4: Write the Intro — the 5-Part Hook

The intro must confirm the click within the first lines (click confirmation: restate and ideally exceed the expectation the title set), then open a curiosity loop. Use this 5-part structure in order (parts 4 and 5 can swap):

1. **Context**: Bluntly state what the video is about, as fast as possible, confirming the click.
2. **Common belief**: Restate the conventional take on the topic. This builds common ground (the viewer likely holds this belief) and sets up the contrast.
3. **Contrarian approach**: Contradict the common belief with a take or method they haven't heard. This is what stuns them into staying.
4. **Plan**: Give the ordered plan — the steps or list the video will walk through.
5. **Proof**: Establish credibility for why they should trust this new approach (results, experience, analysis performed).

**Hook Commandments** (verify all 4):
1. Alignment: Visual, spoken, and text hooks match
2. Speed: Value promised in first 3 seconds
3. Clarity: Topic is unmistakable
4. Curiosity: Opens a question they must answer

### Checkpoint 5: Write the Body

**Ordering — second-best first**: Put your second-best point first and your best point second. The first point after the intro must genuinely surprise (this is where most viewers bounce if it's generic), and going 1→2 in ascending value trains the viewer that value keeps climbing, so they stay. Remaining points follow in descending order. This is the same reason albums put the single in track 3 or 4, not track 1.

For each point, use the **Value Loop**:
1. **Context**: What is it? (Simple, clear, digestible)
2. **Application**: How to do it — with as many relevant, concrete examples as possible
3. **Framing**: Why it matters and how it fits the overall puzzle

Alternate zoom-in (tactics) and zoom-out (big picture) like a wave — it aids comprehension and lets viewers extract tactics while following the full story.

**Transitions — rehook between every point**: Never jump flatly from one point to the next. End each point with a mini-hook that makes the next one mandatory, e.g. "That point matters, but without the next one the whole thing falls apart." Attention decays continuously; rehooks (every 30-60 seconds) reset it. Setups & payoffs work too: tease information, delay the reveal, then deliver.

### Checkpoint 6: Outro — End on a High Note

The outro is not an afterthought: it drives likes, shares, comments, and rewatches.

1. **Summarize** the points covered and remind the viewer of the pain point that was solved — the final proof that reality beat expectations (like the free chocolate that arrives with the restaurant check: a last reminder of the good service).
2. **End on a high note**: leave the lasting impression "that was an amazing video."
3. **Binge loop** (optional, last 15-30 seconds): introduce a NEW question and promise to solve it in another video or resource.

### Checkpoint 7: Native CTA Embeds

Promote products/resources without breaking flow or feeling like an ad:

- During the outline (Checkpoint 3), pick the point(s) that naturally suit the resource as the solve.
- At that moment in the script, either (a) frame the pain point, then offer the resource as the solve, or (b) speedrun a framework so deep/fast that the resource is offered as the way to recap or implement faster.
- The resource must genuinely solve or extend the point being made. Mention it natively, then move on — no tonal shift, no "quick word from our sponsor" energy.

### Checkpoint 8: Edit for Quality

Run four audits on your draft:

**1. Story Flow Audit**
- Read each line: Is this necessary or a tangent?
- Delete anything that breaks the through-line
- Test: Can viewer follow beginning to end without getting lost?

**2. Comprehension Audit**
- Target 6th grade vocabulary
- Shorter sentences (staccato, not run-on)
- Active voice ("The dog jumped" not "The jumping was done by the dog")
- Restate complex ideas twice, second time simpler

**3. Speed-to-Value Audit**
- Is value signaled in first 3 seconds?
- Rehooks every 30-60 seconds?
- Outro summarizes, ends high, and extends value?

**4. AI-ism Audit**
- Hunt for the banned formulations listed in "Writing Style: Avoid AI-isms" and rewrite them as direct statements
- Check the pain point isn't being re-agitated in every section

## Quick Reference: Script Template

```
HOOK (0-15 seconds)
1. Context — confirm the click
2. Common belief
3. Contrarian approach
4. Plan
5. Proof / credibility

BODY SECTION 1 (Content via Value Loop → Rehook)
[Second-best point]

BODY SECTION 2 (Content via Value Loop → Rehook)
[Best point]

BODY SECTION 3+ (Content via Value Loop → Rehook)
[Remaining points in descending order]
[Native CTA embed at the natural point]

OUTRO (Last 15-30 seconds)
- Summarize value delivered, end on a high note
- Optional binge loop to next video/resource
```
