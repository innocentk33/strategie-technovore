# YouTube Script Writing — Cam's voice

Transform research and raw ideas into retention-optimized scripts that sound like Cam: a gamer telling a friend a crazy true story, in spoken French, with constant light humor and solid research underneath.

**Before writing any line of script, read `references/voix-cam.md`.** It contains the voice, the humor mechanics and the narrative toolbox, with annotated examples from Cam's own scripts. The workflow below tells you *what* to write; that file tells you *how it should sound*.

## Two formats

Pick the format during Checkpoint 1 and tell the user which one you're using.

- **Format A — Récit documentaire** (Cam's main format): the story of a company, a game, a person or an event, told chronologically with a protagonist, a rise, reversals and an epilogue. Examples: how Gameloft became "le roi du plagiat" then disappeared; how The Crew's shutdown started Stop Killing Games.
- **Format B — Éducatif / liste**: a video that teaches a method or makes an argument in points. Same voice, different skeleton.

If unsure, default to Format A when the subject has a timeline and characters, Format B when the viewer's goal is to learn how to do something.

## Core Psychology

All great scripts operate on one principle: **Reality must beat expectations.**

- Viewer expectations = what they think will happen based on title/thumbnail
- Reality = what you actually deliver in the video
- When reality > expectations → satisfaction, engagement, shares
- When reality < expectations → disappointment, drop-off, bounce

Every decision in scriptwriting serves this gap. You cannot beat expectations if you don't know what they are — which is why packaging comes before writing. In a story video, beating expectations mostly means: facts the viewer didn't know, reversals they didn't see coming, and a clearer "why" than any article gives them.

## Writing Style

### Cam's voice in one paragraph
Spoken French only (no "ne", "on" instead of "nous", left dislocations like "Michel, il a la vision"), narration in the present with the near future ("ils vont sortir…"), oral connectors (alors, bon, et ben, en gros, sauf que, forcément), familiar vocabulary in moderation, a short punchline after every long build-up, a joke every 20–40 seconds, and characters instead of entities. Full detail and examples in `references/voix-cam.md`.

### Avoid AI-isms
Scripts must sound like a human creator talking, not like generated text. **Never use these formulations** (in English or French):

- Negation-contrast constructions: "It's not because A, but because B" / "Pas parce que A, mais parce que B"
- "It's not just X, it's Y" / "C'est pas juste Y, c'est X"
- Absolution openers: "It's not your fault, it's..." / "C'est pas de ta faute, c'est..."
- Repeated, heavy-handed hammering of "pain points" — agitating the viewer's problem once is a hook; doing it every paragraph is a tell

Also avoid: rule-of-three lists everywhere, "Here's the thing:", "Let that sink in", em-dash-heavy dramatic pauses, symmetrical parallel sentences stacked back to back, and written-French connectors ("Néanmoins", "En effet", "Ainsi,"). Prefer plain, direct statements, varied sentence length, and concrete examples over rhetorical scaffolding.

## The Workflow

Work through these checkpoints in order. Each builds on the previous. **Steps 1–3 (packaging, research, outline) happen before writing a single line of script.** Measure twice, cut once: if the idea or outline isn't strong, don't write the script — go back and research more. Show the user the output of each checkpoint and get a go-ahead before moving on, unless they ask for the full script in one shot.

### Checkpoint 1: Packaging (Idea, Title, Thumbnail, Format)

The packaging sets the viewer's expectations, and the script's job is to confirm and then beat them.

1. **Idea**: One sentence describing the video. It should either solve a real pain point for the ideal viewer or open a rabbit hole they want to go down. For Format A, phrase it as a paradox or a question ("Le studio qui a copié tout le monde sans jamais aller au tribunal, et qui a quand même disparu").
2. **Title**: Must be locked BEFORE writing the intro. The title triggers the click-curiosity loop and defines the incoming expectation. Slight wording tweaks change the expectation completely — you can't write click confirmation without knowing the exact title.
3. **Thumbnail**: A loose concept is enough at this stage. Don't let thumbnail iteration block scriptwriting; it gets dialed in later.
4. **Target audience & desired emotion**: Who specifically is watching, what do they already know, and which ONE primary emotion is the video transferring (awe, amusement, excitement, outrage, surprise/curiosity, empathy, nostalgia)?
5. **Format**: A (récit) or B (éducatif), see above.

### Checkpoint 2: Research & Mine for Shock

Find the facts that will beat expectations. Use web search when available; the voice is casual but the facts must be exact (dates, figures, names, order of events). Note sources for the user.

**Shock Score Method**: Rate each fact 1-100 based on "How many viewers would NOT know this?"
- 80+ = Gold (most viewers surprised)
- 50-79 = Good supporting points
- Below 50 = Skip unless essential context

Collect 5-10 high-shock facts before proceeding. For Format A, also collect:
- **Characters**: who wants what, who is frustrated, who is the antagonist.
- **Reversals**: every moment where things flipped — these become your "Sauf que".
- **Hidden links**: a famous name that can be revealed late (e.g. the farming family that turns out to be the Guillemots of Ubisoft).
- **Tangible comparisons**: ways to make numbers physical (the thumbnail of this very video weighs more than the phone's storage; two maps side by side).
- **Concepts to explain**: legal/technical/financial ideas the viewer needs (OPA, emulation, free-to-play economics).

### Checkpoint 3: Outline First — the Uniqueness Gut-Check

Before any prose, write a quick-and-dirty bulleted outline. The intro is formulaic and can always be solved later; the real moat of a video is whether the body says something genuinely unique.

**Format A outline — chapters by era or phase.** For each chapter:
- **Situation**: where the protagonist stands at the start
- **Want / problem**: what they try to do, what blocks them
- **Reversal**: the "Sauf que" that ends the chapter and pulls into the next
- **Detour** (optional): the concept to explain, with its analogy
- **Joke seeds**: 2–3 comic angles (imagined scene, absurd recap, reference)

Then define the **running motif** (a line that recurs and evolves, like "Nouveau plan : conquérir X") and at least one **callback metaphor** planted early and paid off late. Check the arc: setup → rise → peak ("le prime") → fall or twist → epilogue.

**Format B outline — points.** For each bullet, layer on:
- **What**: what the point is
- **Why**: why it matters
- **How**: how it fits into the overall story

Then zoom out and gut-check: **is this actually unique, or am I summarizing what everyone already says?** Uniqueness can come from the facts, the angle, the way it's distilled, or the example used. If it isn't unique, do NOT proceed to writing: research more and come back. Also mark where a native CTA fits (see Checkpoint 7).

### Checkpoint 4: Write the Intro

The intro must confirm the click within the first lines, then open a curiosity loop. No channel greeting: the video starts on the hook.

**Format A — Cam's story hook** (≈ 30–45 s):
1. **Cold open**: either the *pattern + exception* (2–3 dated facts that set a rule, then "Et pourtant, il existe une exception") or the *second-person hypothetical* ("Imaginez que…").
2. **The subject, framed as a paradox**: one or two sentences with the most striking contrast of the story ("numéro 1 incontestable du jeu mobile avant de totalement disparaître des radars").
3. **The central question or the promise**: "Alors, comment ça se fait et pourquoi…?" or "Entre X, Y et Z, aujourd'hui je vous raconte l'histoire de…".
4. **The launch into the flashback**: "Pour répondre à cette question, il va falloir revenir des années plus tôt…" — ideally somewhere unexpected, with a delayed reveal.

**Format B — the 5-part hook** (parts 4 and 5 can swap):
1. **Context**: Bluntly state what the video is about, as fast as possible, confirming the click.
2. **Common belief**: Restate the conventional take on the topic.
3. **Contrarian approach**: Contradict the common belief with a take the viewer hasn't heard.
4. **Plan**: Give the ordered plan the video will walk through.
5. **Proof**: Establish credibility (results, experience, analysis performed).

**Hook Commandments** (verify all 4, both formats):
1. Alignment: Visual, spoken, and text hooks match
2. Speed: Value promised in first 3 seconds
3. Clarity: Topic is unmistakable
4. Curiosity: Opens a question they must answer

### Checkpoint 5: Write the Body

**Format A — chapter mechanics**
- Open each chapter on a stable situation, end it on a reversal ("La vie est belle et pourtant…", "Sauf que…"). The reversal is the rehook.
- Drive the plot through the protagonist's wants and frustrations, not through corporate events.
- Use the toolbox from `references/voix-cam.md`: voiced viewer questions, mystery → false lead → answer, absurdity recaps, imagined scenes, the running motif. Spread them; don't use the same device twice in a row.
- Pedagogical detours: announce ("Pour faire simple"), explain with an analogy or a consequence for "toi", make numbers physical, return to the story with a hook.
- Keep a rehook every 30–60 seconds of speech: a reversal, a question, a tease ("Mais ça, on n'y est pas encore").
- At the emotional peak (a loss, an injustice), pause the jokes for a few lines.

**Format B — ordering and value loops**
- **Second-best first**: put your second-best point first and your best point second, then the rest in descending order. The first point must genuinely surprise; ascending value 1→2 trains the viewer that value keeps climbing.
- For each point, use the **Value Loop**: Context (what it is) → Application (how, with concrete examples) → Framing (why it matters).
- Alternate zoom-in (tactics) and zoom-out (big picture) like a wave.
- **Rehook between every point**: end each point with a mini-hook that makes the next one mandatory. Setups & payoffs work too: tease, delay, deliver.

### Checkpoint 6: Outro — End on a High Note

The outro is not an afterthought: it drives likes, shares, comments, and rewatches.

**Format A**
1. **Epilogue**: "Et c'est vrai ça, que sont-ils devenus ?" — close every character's arc, with a last callback to the running motif.
2. **Nuanced verdict**: what the story means, fair to all sides, linked to the viewer's own memories.
3. **Plain outro**: "Dites-moi ce que vous en pensez. J'espère que ça vous a plu. Si c'est le cas, abonnez-vous. C'était Camubx." — or a **cliffhanger toward part 2** that reframes the viewer's question into a better one.

**Format B**
1. **Summarize** the points covered and remind the viewer of the problem that was solved — the final proof that reality beat expectations.
2. **End on a high note**: leave the lasting impression "that was an amazing video."
3. **Binge loop** (optional, last 15-30 seconds): introduce a NEW question and promise to solve it in another video.

### Checkpoint 7: Native CTA Embeds

Promote products/resources without breaking flow or feeling like an ad:

- During the outline (Checkpoint 3), pick the moment that naturally suits the resource.
- Frame the problem, then offer the resource as the solve — or, in a story, tie it to what just happened in the plot.
- Keep Cam's voice through the CTA: same register, a joke if it fits, then straight back to the story. No "petit mot de notre sponsor" tonal break.

### Checkpoint 8: Edit for Quality

Run five audits on your draft:

**1. Story Flow Audit**
- Read each line: Is this necessary or a tangent?
- Delete anything that breaks the through-line
- Test: Can viewer follow beginning to end without getting lost?

**2. Comprehension Audit**
- Simple vocabulary, short sentences, active voice
- Every concept the viewer needs is explained once, simply, with an analogy
- Restate complex ideas twice, second time simpler

**3. Speed-to-Value Audit**
- Is value signaled in the first 3 seconds?
- Rehook every 30-60 seconds?
- Does the outro close the loops and end high?

**4. AI-ism Audit**
- Hunt for the banned formulations listed in "Avoid AI-isms" and rewrite them as direct statements
- Check the outrage or pain point isn't being re-agitated in every section

**5. Cam Voice Audit** (check against `references/voix-cam.md`, section 9 dosage table)
- Read it aloud: does every sentence sound spoken? Any "ne", "nous", "néanmoins" left?
- Is there a joke roughly every 20–40 seconds, and none explained?
- Is the running motif present and paid off at the end?
- Does each chapter end on a reversal?
- Are Cam's personal moments (memory, opinion, theory) present but limited to 2–4?
- Are all facts, dates and figures accurate? Nothing invented for a gag?

## Script Output Format

Write the script as continuous spoken text, broken into chapters, with production cues on their own lines in brackets:
- `[VISUEL: …]` for anything the joke or explanation depends on (a meme, a map comparison, a face reaction)
- `[MUSIQUE: …]` for mood changes
- `[SFX: …]` for sound effects

Give an estimated duration at the end (≈ 150 words per minute of speech).

### Format A template
```
TITRE / MINIATURE / ÉMOTION CIBLE

COLD OPEN (0:00–0:45)
- Pattern + exception, ou hypothèse "Imaginez que…"
- Le paradoxe du sujet
- La question centrale / la promesse
- Lancement du flashback

CHAPITRE 1 — [époque / phase]
Situation → envie / problème → détour pédago (option) → "Sauf que…"

CHAPITRE 2…N — [époque / phase]
Même mécanique, le motif récurrent revient et évolue
Le pic ("le prime") → la chute ou le twist

ÉPILOGUE
"Que sont-ils devenus ?" → verdict nuancé → outro simple ou cliffhanger
```

### Format B template
```
HOOK (0-15 seconds)
1. Context — confirm the click
2. Common belief
3. Contrarian approach
4. Plan
5. Proof / credibility

BODY SECTION 1 — [second-best point] (Value Loop → Rehook)
BODY SECTION 2 — [best point] (Value Loop → Rehook)
BODY SECTION 3+ — [remaining points, descending] (Value Loop → Rehook)
[Native CTA embed at the natural point]

OUTRO (last 15-30 seconds)
- Summarize, end on a high note
- Optional binge loop
```

# La voix de Cam — guide de ton, d'humour et de narration

Distilled from two reference scripts (the story of Gameloft, "le roi du plagiat"; the story of The Crew, "le plus beau fiasco du jeu vidéo"). Read this whole file before writing any line of a script. ## 1. Who is talking

A gamer in his twenties who was a teenager in the 2010s, telling a friend a crazy true story he's genuinely obsessed with. He's done the research, he knows the dates and the numbers, but he tells it like a conversation, not a lecture.

Emotional stance:
- **Admiration** for visionaries, underdogs and people who take insane risks (Michel Guillemot "a la vision", the modders of TCU).
- **Irony** toward big corporations, billionaires and bad decisions (Bolloré as "un insatiable très très gros requin", Atari refusing the project).
- **Indignation** when something is unfair, but channeled into storytelling, never a sermon (The Crew shutdown).
- **Fairness**: he gives the other side its due ("qu'on les aime ou pas", "c'est dans leur droit, ça serait terrible pour leur image").

## 2. Register and syntax

Spoken French, written to be read aloud. If a sentence sounds like a Wikipedia article or a press release, rewrite it.

**Grammar of the spoken register**
- Drop the "ne": "ça va pas", "il y a pas", "personne les attendait".
- "On" instead of "nous"; "ça" everywhere.
- Left dislocation, constantly: "Michel, il a la vision." / "Ce truc, il a 300 ko de stockage." / "Ubisoft, bah c'est surtout beaucoup de dettes."
- Narration in the present, with the near future to push the story forward: "En 2006, ils vont sortir…", "Ça va super bien marcher, évidemment."
- Short punch sentence after a long build-up: "Ça c'est du prime." / "Un point c'est tout." / "Game over." / "Wouh !"

**Oral connectors (the rhythm of the voice)**
Alors, Bon, Et ben, Bah, Voilà, OK, En gros, Grosso modo, Pour faire simple, Ni une ni deux, Forcément, Évidemment, Sauf que, Et pourtant, Du coup, Faut dire que, Je veux dire.

"Sauf que" is the single most important word in the voice: it's the hinge of almost every reversal.

**Familiar vocabulary bank** (use naturally, 1–2 per paragraph, never in every sentence)
boîte, tune, pognon, gugus, compères, de ouf, fou furieux, dinguerie, vachement, hyper, genre, balek, le seum, un carton, ça va tout péter, se faire défoncer / allumer, cravacher, cruncher, flexer, se pointer, se barrer, péter de tune, être dans le mal.
Stronger words ("la merde", "partir en couille", "emmerder") exist in the voice but stay rare: a handful per video, used for comic emphasis, never aggressive.

## 3. Narrative devices (the toolbox)

Each device below is a mechanism. A good script uses most of them, spread across the video; none of them should appear more than two or three times.

**3.1 Cold open: the pattern and its exception**
Stack two or three dated, concrete facts that establish a rule, then break it.
> "Septembre 2024, Nintendo porte plainte contre Palworld… 2018, PUBG attaque Fortnite… Si vous copiez le jeu de quelqu'un d'autre, ça finira quasi systématiquement au tribunal. Et pourtant, il existe une exception."

**3.2 Cold open: the second-person hypothetical**
Put the viewer inside the situation before naming the subject.
> "Imaginez que votre jeu préféré soit supprimé. Du jour au lendemain…"

**3.3 The question and the promise**
Right after the cold open, state the central question(s) the video answers, or announce the flavor of the story in a single sentence, then start. No "salut à tous", no channel intro.
> "Alors, comment ça se fait et pourquoi celui qu'on surnomma le roi du plagiat a fini par tomber de son trône ?"
> "Entre idée révolutionnaire, influence politique et tentative de résurrection, aujourd'hui je vous raconte l'histoire du plus beau fiasco que le jeu vidéo ait connu."

**3.4 The flashback with a delayed reveal**
Start the story somewhere that seems unrelated, say so openly, and reveal the famous name later as a mini twist.
> "…une petite boîte bretonne qui vend des produits agricoles. Pour l'instant, il y a aucun lien avec le jeu vidéo, mais ça va vite changer." … "leur nom de famille c'est Guillemot et l'entreprise qu'ils viennent de créer, elle s'appelle Ubisoft."

**3.5 Characters, not entities**
Stories run on people with motives. Introduce the protagonist like a character in a film ("Voici Michel, le cadet et geek de la famille."), give groups affectionate nicknames ("nos trois gugus", "nos trois compères", "Wami et son équipe de cracks"), and drive the plot through what they want and what frustrates them ("il y en a un, il est pas content, c'est Michel").

**3.6 The running motif**
Give each video one recurring line that evolves with the story and becomes a callback. In Gameloft: "il a la vision" and "Nouveau plan : conquérir [le web / le mobile / le smartphone / le free-to-play / l'IA]". Also recurring metaphors that come back transformed: "les petits poissons qui grossissent, ça attire les requins" → later "les gros poissons affaiblis, c'est leur plat préféré". Plan the motif during the outline.

**3.7 Every chapter turns on a "Sauf que"**
Set up a stable or happy situation, then flip it. The flip is the rehook.
> "La vie est belle et pourtant il y en a un, il est pas content."
> "La fusée Gameloft a réussi son décollage. Direction la lune. Sauf qu'elle va exploser en vol."
> "Et ça aurait dû être pareil pour 2010, sauf que 2010, c'est l'arrivée d'Android."

**3.8 Mystery → false lead → answer**
Pose the puzzle dramatically, deflate the obvious explanation, then give the real one.
> "Quel genre de catastrophe nucléaire peut à ce point détruire une boîte en une seule année ? Rien, rien du tout. […] Et ben, la réponse, on la trouve quand on regarde les concurrents."

**3.9 Voicing the viewer's question**
Say out loud the question the viewer has at that moment, then answer it.
> "Normalement, vous devez vous poser la même question que moi : mais comment c'est possible qu'ils se soient pas déjà pris 15 procès ?"
> "Alors qu'est-ce qu'Ubisoft a à voir avec Gameloft ? Et ben absolument tout, vous allez voir."

**3.10 The absurdity recap**
After listing several obstacles, recap them in one breath to show how insane the plan is, then land a reaction.
> "Si je récapitule là, le projet c'est faire des jeux extrêmement légers, parfaits techniquement, négociés auprès de tous les opérateurs de tous les pays […] Tout ça pour à la fin toucher 30 % des gains. Wouh ! Et c'est là-dedans que Michel a choisi d'aller."

**3.11 Imagined scenes and fake dialogues**
Stage a meeting or a pitch as a mini sketch, with obvious exaggeration.
> "Ils ont dû se pointer chez Ubisoft, lunettes de soleil, petit costard, en disant : 'Vous voyez ce jeu qui a changé la façon dont on perçoit les jeux de voiture […] ? Bah c'est nous qui l'avons fait.'"
> "Et là se pointe Eden Games. — Nous, on a pris Hawaï. — Comment ça vous avez pris une ville hawaïenne ? — Non, on a pris l'île d'Hawaï à l'échelle 1:1."

**3.12 The absurd option list**
List the possible solutions, make the first ones ridiculous or dark, then pivot back with a "je déconne".
> "La première, contacter un hacker […] lui coller un flingue contre la tempe. La deuxième, plus pragmatique, louer un camion […] cambrioler la salle des serveurs. Et la dernière, recréer les serveurs soi-même. […] Et donc tout naturellement, Wami prend son flingue et… Non, je déconne."

**3.13 Scene-setting with the era**
Anchor the viewer in the time period with a concrete, nostalgic detail rather than a date alone: the operator portal, the Crazy Frog ringtone "pour devenir le plus cool du collège pendant deux semaines", "un gadget pour jeune cadre dynamique pété de tune".

## 4. Humor: what kind, how often

The humor is constant but light: a joke every 20–40 seconds of speech, most of them one line, never slowing the story. Kinds that work:

- **Understatement / deflation**: "Ils ont choisi la deuxième option." / "Bon, ils vont choisir la deuxième option."
- **Hyperbole with gaming or anime vocabulary**: "Passage en mode Super Saiyan… mode Gear 5", "c'est le prime", "échange équivalent, tout ce qu'il y a de plus standard pour tout bon alchimiste qui se respecte".
- **Pop-culture and French internet references**, quick and unexplained: films, anime, memes, bac philo ("Vous avez 3h. Bon courage."), OSS 117 ("Pas mal non ? C'est français.").
- **Self-deprecation and self-awareness**: "Alors non, c'est pas ma chaîne, même si vous devriez vous y abonner." / the teenage diaporama story "j'ai eu 18, ça valait le coup".
- **Visual gags written into the script**: "Grosso modo, les yeux de Michel, ils doivent être comme ça." → always add a `[VISUEL: …]` cue so the editor knows.
- **Dark or edgy jokes** are allowed if clearly absurd and immediately defused ("Non, je déconne").

Never explain a joke. Never stack two jokes on the same fact. If the story is at an emotional peak (someone loses their life's work, a community mourns its game), drop the jokes for a few lines and let it land.

## 5. Pedagogy: explaining complex stuff

Cam regularly stops the story to explain a technical, legal or financial concept (the mobile market in 2001, an OPA, why cloning ideas is legal, what an emulator does, why you can't shrink a map). The pattern:

1. Announce the detour casually: "Pour faire simple…", "En gros…", "Là il faut que je vous parle de…"
2. Explain with a concrete analogy or a consequence for "toi": the OPA is explained through what happens to *your* shares if a madman buys the company; the emulator is "exactement comme parler avec un extraterrestre".
3. Make numbers physical: "300 ko… la miniature de cette vidéo prend genre cinq fois plus de place"; show two maps side by side instead of quoting km².
4. Return to the story with a hook.

Rigor matters: dates, figures, names and sequences must be accurate and sourced during research. When something is uncertain, say it in the voice ("Alors, je sais pas s'ils avaient vraiment pas l'argent ou s'ils voulaient juste pas prendre le risque, mais…") instead of inventing.

## 6. Opinion and personal presence

Cam is present in the story, in small doses (2–4 moments per video):
- Personal memory: "Je me souviens quand j'étais ado, ça me rendait dingue…"
- Clearly labeled opinion: "À mon humble avis, aujourd'hui il reste le meilleur trailer de jeu vidéo."
- A theory offered for debate: "On pourra en débattre en commentaire, mais ma théorie c'est que…"
- Addressing the viewer directly: "Je vous préviens, ça va aller très vite." / "Accrochez-vous parce qu'on vient à peine de commencer."

He can pick a side, but he argues it (anticipating the counter-argument: "Vous pourrez me dire que oui mais GTA 6…").

## 7. Endings

- **Epilogue**: "Et c'est vrai ça, que sont-ils devenus ?" — tie up every character, including the protagonist's next "plan" (a final callback to the running motif).
- **Nuanced verdict**: what the story means, with warmth and a link to the viewer's own memories ("C'est même fortement probable qu'un de leurs jeux soit un de vos jeux nostalgiques d'enfance").
- **Plain outro**: "Dites-moi ce que vous en pensez. J'espère que ça vous a plu. Si c'est le cas, abonnez-vous. C'était Camubx." Short, no begging.
- **Or a cliffhanger toward part 2**: reframe the question the viewer has been asking into a better one.
  > "Je suis sûr que vous vous êtes posé la question : 'Est-ce qu'ils vont réussir ?' Moi, j'aurais plutôt demandé : 'Est-ce qu'ils ont réussi ?' Ouais, ça c'est une meilleure question."

## 8. What Cam never does

- Opening with "Salut à tous, c'est Cam, aujourd'hui on va parler de…" — the video starts with the cold open.
- Guru / growth-hacker tone ("Dans cette vidéo je vais vous révéler 5 secrets…").
- Moralizing lectures or repeating the outrage every paragraph.
- Stacking hype adjectives ("incroyable, fou, dingue, légendaire").
- Long quotations read from articles.
- Written-French constructions: "Néanmoins", "En effet", "Il convient de", "Ainsi," at the start of sentences, subjunctive-heavy sentences.
- Inventing facts, quotes or numbers for a better joke.

## 9. Dosage cheat-sheet (for a ~20-minute script)

| Running motif / callbacks | 1 motif, 3–6 occurrences |
| "Sauf que" reversals | 1 per chapter minimum |
| Voiced viewer question | 2–4 |
| Imagined scene / fake dialogue | 1–3 |
